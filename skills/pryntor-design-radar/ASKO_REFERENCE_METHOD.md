## What actually worked in Asko V2
1. **Product problem first:** local source inspection needs to feel understandable and safe. The visual direction makes navigating unfamiliar code less daunting instead of merely ornamenting the interface.
2. **Multiple references → one new authored world:** illustration, editorial type, lilac/periwinkle/mint/pink/warm-yellow applications, a new human guide Ari and her black cat. Nothing is a copied branded screen.
3. **Product-specific character:** original responsive hand-authored SVG; Ari responds to keyboard/click/hover and respects reduced motion. It is not an interchangeable cartoon slapped onto any company.
4. **The artwork connects to a real feature:** Ari opens a native accessible guide; helpful answers are deterministic and tied only to a user's local scan/report. No fake LLM, remote service, unrelated claims.
5. **Every major CTA works:** upload owned files, try sample, inspect import map/findings, use keyboard palette, export local report. Sample content is truthfully labelled SAMPLE.
6. **Prove experience, not just screenshots:** real desktop/mobile browser actions, no JS errors, no unexpected external requests, no horizontal overflow, 23/23 hosted tests and zero automatic axe WCAG A/AA violations across six assessed states. Incomplete human accessibility/security review still noted.
7. **Preserve the prior version:** V0/V1 separate, approved V2 frozen, experiments in independent preview; founder creative acceptance is separate from commercial/security signoff.

## The reusable Asko Method (the method itself, not its aesthetic)
**Step 1. Define the user's meaningful job.** Describe their start, uncertainty, decision and tangible outcome in one sentence. Identify the specific visual role that will help.
**Step 2. Read original reference cases.** For each source save name, link/creator, date when available, screenshot or accessible evidence, key interaction/visual mechanism, emotional effect and why it helps the product. Distinguish observed behavior from vendor claims.
**Step 3. Extract mechanisms, not visuals.** Separate `what it does for a user` from `how the reference looks`. No logo/palette/layout/logo tracing, trademark borrowing, or unattributed assets.
**Step 4. Combine into an original product metaphor.** Use the user's company identity, context and constraints. Compare at least three genuinely different territories with screenshots, story, new illustration/assets, interactive role, references and cost/rights implications. Avoid arbitrary 3D sculpture or stock SaaS kits.
**Step 5. Build the useful interaction with the visual.** It should teach, navigate, reveal, validate, demonstrate or respond to real state. Effects without functional or narrative reason do not count toward design acceptance.
**Step 6. Make every obvious action real.** Real example path, real result and empty/error states, accurate limitations and privacy. Don't fake backend AI, approvals, booking, shipping or other outcomes.
**Step 7. Show the product in context.** Desktop, iPhone touch, small-screen type, short-session user flows, keyboard and reduced-motion, slow network, visual snapshots and contrast. Keep tests traceable to the source/head and save a before-and-after. Distinguish technically green from founder art-direction acceptance and independent human QA.
**Step 8. Freeze what was accepted.** No silent overwrite, use a separate source branch/preview, get founder visual approval before production promotion; verify rollback and spending permissions separately.

## Nonnegotiable product acceptance rubric (0 / 1 / 2 each)
- **Specificity** — could this illustration/flow belong to any app, or only this product?
- **Originality & rights** — actual authored world rather than copied surface/third-party template?
- **Utility** — the hero, illustration and motion help a task, not just entertain?
- **Coherence** — character, type, hierarchy, colours, microinteraction and product copy form one grammar?
- **Proof** — real working actions, accurate capability claims and meaningful edge cases?
- **Delivery** — mobile, keyboard, reduced motion, privacy, performance, regression testing, prior baseline protected?
Score >=10/12 is a **proposal review threshold** only; never auto-approve security or founder taste. Founder acceptance is explicit, not inferred from CI green.

## Scope by venture
- **Pryntor Launch Desk:** a distinctive **evidence and decision experience**, interactive record/receipt or product-relevant original guide; motion tied to reviewed gates and actual evidence, not looping abstract geometry. Real export; distinguish self-report from independent approval.
- **LVL7 & Show Your Working:** original learning characters/diagrams where they make mathematical thinking or next steps clearer; teaching aids are never grading authority.
- **BTWW & Studios:** human-authored cinematic editorial worlds and motion tied to music/culture; maintain rights and credits.
- **Tirisano:** premium, human-first travel coordination; not a playful kids mascot or generic tourism image.
- **Recover, Mission Control, robotics:** restrained purposeful visual model, honest system actions, explanations and confidence states; safety limitations remain visible.
